What Is a Phishing Email?

What Is the Goal of a Phishing Email?

The main purpose of a phishing email is to get you to take an action that benefits the attacker. This can include:

  • Clicking on a link that takes you to a fake website (that steals your password)
  • Downloading an attachment that installs malware (harmful software)
  • Entering your details on a spoofed login page
  • Transferring money or sharing sensitive company data
  • Giving away personal information like credit card numbers or Social Security numbers

How Do Phishing Emails Trick You?

Phishing emails are often designed to look very convincing. Here’s how they try to fool you:

  • They pretend to be someone you trust – like your company’s HR department, your bank, or an online service you use (e.g., Amazon, PayPal).
  • They create a sense of urgency – such as "Your account will be suspended in 24 hours" or "There has been suspicious activity on your account."
  • They include links or attachments – usually leading to fake websites or containing viruses.
  • They use scare tactics or emotional pressure – to get you to act quickly without thinking.

Common Signs of a Phishing Email

You can spot a phishing email by looking for these red flags:

Red Flag What to Look For
Unknown or strange sender Email is from someone you don’t know or a suspicious address
Urgent or threatening language “Act now”, “Immediate response required”, etc.
Generic greetings “Dear Customer” instead of your name
Poor spelling/grammar Many phishing emails have typos or awkward phrasing
Links don’t match Hover over the link to see where it really goes
Unexpected attachments Files you didn’t ask for or weren’t expecting

Phishing Today

Phishing can arrive through email, text messages, social media, or phone calls. A familiar name or professional design does not guarantee that a message is genuine.

  • Fake workplace requests: A message may impersonate your manager or IT team to ask for a payment, login, or sensitive file.
  • QR-code phishing: A QR code can hide a link to a fake sign-in page. Treat unexpected QR codes with the same care as unexpected links.
  • Personalized messages: Scammers may use details about your work or interests to make a request feel familiar.

Learn more: Canadian Centre for Cyber Security: recognizing phishing and U.S. Postal Inspection Service: QR-code phishing.

How AI Can Make Phishing More Convincing

Criminals can use generative AI to write convincing messages faster, improve spelling and grammar, translate emails, and tailor requests to a target. A polished email can still be a scam.

AI can also imitate voices or create fake videos to support an impersonation attempt. A follow-up call that sounds familiar is not enough to verify a suspicious email.

These risks are described in the FBI guidance on AI-assisted fraud.

Check the Request, Not Just the Writing

  • Check the full sender address and link destination. A familiar display name is not proof of identity.
  • For an unexpected login request, open the official app or use a saved website address instead of the message’s link.
  • Verify payment or sensitive-data requests using a contact method you already trust, such as a known phone number.
  • Pause when a message pressures you to act quickly or keep the request secret. Report suspicious messages to your security team.

What Should You Do If You Receive a Phishing Email?

If you think an email might be phishing, don’t click anything. Here’s what to do instead:

  • Do not reply to the email
  • Do not click any links or download any attachments
  • Report the email to your IT department or email provider
  • Delete the email right away

If you accidentally clicked a link or entered your information:

  • Change your passwords immediately
  • Contact your IT/security team
  • Monitor your accounts for unusual activity

How to Stay Safe from Phishing Emails

  • Always double-check the sender’s address
  • Be cautious with links and attachments
  • Use strong, unique passwords for each account
  • Turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA)
  • Stay updated with cybersecurity awareness training
